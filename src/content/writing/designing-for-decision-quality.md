Good interface design does not begin with arranging components. It begins with
understanding the decision someone is trying to make.

## Start with the decision

Document the choice, the evidence needed to make it, and what happens after the
choice is made. This keeps the interface centered on useful outcomes instead of
surface polish.

## Design the evidence

Hierarchy, comparison, defaults, and progressive disclosure should help people
evaluate information without hiding uncertainty.

## Measure confidence, not clicks

Completion rate matters, but so do reversals, errors, support requests, and the
confidence people report after making a consequential choice.
